# Rebuilding My SaaS Onboarding — Build in Public

## The Problem

Launched Flashletter a while ago. **1 user tried it.**

Looking at my signup flow, I realized why:

- Landing page → Sign up page → Create account → Verify email → Finally try the product

That's 4 steps before anyone sees value. In 2025, that's a death sentence.

## The Realization

Modern successful products flip this:

- **Figma**: Jump straight into design, sign up when you want to save
- **Notion**: Start writing immediately
- **Linear**: Create issues before creating an account

The pattern: **Let them feel value FIRST, ask for commitment AFTER.**

## What I Changed

**Before:**

```
[Landing Page] → Click "Start Creating"
                      ↓
              [Redirected to Login]
                      ↓
         [Sign Up with Email or Google]
                      ↓
            [Now you can try the product]
```

User thinks: "Do I really want to give my email to this random site just to see if it works?"

**After:**

```
[Landing Page] → Click "Try Free — No Sign Up"
                      ↓
           [Instantly on the create page]
                      ↓
        [Enter your URL, generate campaign]
                      ↓
     [Love it? Sign up to save. Don't? Just leave.]
```

User thinks: "Oh cool, let me just try it real quick."

One button. Zero friction. Instant value.

## How It Works

1. Visitor clicks "Try Free — No Sign Up"
2. They get an anonymous session (no email, no password)
3. They can create 1 full campaign
4. When they want more, they link their Google account
5. All their work is preserved

## Why This Matters

- **Reduces fear**: No commitment required to explore
- **Shows value first**: They experience the product before deciding
- **Natural conversion**: Sign up becomes "save my work" not "trust this random site"

## The Bet

I'm betting that more people will:

1. Actually try the product (current: 1 person 😅)
2. Convert at higher rates (because they already see the value)
3. Have better retention (they signed up knowing what they're getting)
